## The work

Sisyfos is a poetic sculpture that, in its method, material, form and symbolism alike, tells a story of being poised, at one and the same time, on the knife-edge between balance and movement.

I carved the sculpture by hand over four months in the iconic Carrara marble, from a 3.5-tonne block down to 450 kg. Its shape is the geometric form known as the ‘oloid’: a highly efficient form that can roll endlessly, as in the Greek myth of Sisyphus, who must push a stone ball up a hill only to see it roll back down every time he reaches the top.

Unlike the sphere, the oloid was only invented as a form in 1929. Visually it has clear lines and is round at the same time. This geometric paradox is possible because the form consists of two congruent circles, set into each other’s circumference, which give the sculpture its ability to roll, with a distribution of weight that is more efficient than that of the sphere.

In this way, the sculpture rewrites the bleak myth of Sisyphus’ endless labour. The marble stands for the home of both the sculptural tradition and the myth in ancient Greece, while the encounter with the form points to human development and innovation, symbols of progress and of hope for the future. The sculpture also echoes and connects to the artistic legacy of the oloid’s creator, the artist Paul Schatz, whom artists such as Ólafur Elíasson also reference.

## Practical details

The sculpture can stand indoors or outdoors. It can roll, but only rocks slightly if given a gentle push, so it generally stands heavy and stable because of the weight of the marble. A plinth can be made and adapted on installation. The sculpture needs little or no maintenance. The work was exhibited at Charlottenborg in spring 2023 and is now kept in my studio in Copenhagen.
